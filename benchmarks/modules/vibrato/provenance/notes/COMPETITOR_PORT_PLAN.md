# Competitor port plan: extrema, STFT-of-f0, and F0-free spectrogram methods

> Historical design record. Names, paths, aliases, and commands in this note
> predate the class-centered refactor and are intentionally not live APIs.

Four published methods across three families need to enter the harness. One family is already represented but by an unattributed reproduction that has to be replaced; two are absent. A fourth family, FM/AM cross-correlation, is explicitly declined below rather than deferred. This plan specifies how to port each faithfully and states what has to change in the shared harness first.

| --- | --- | --- |
| Extrema / half-cycle measurement | **Prame (1994, 1997)** for the parameter convention, **Rossignol et al. ## Declined: FM/AM cross-correlation (von Coler and Röbel, 2011)

Not ported, and not deferred either. Two reasons, both independent of the paper-access problem that previously blocked it.

**It is already dominated in the published record.** Yang's Table 3 evaluates von Coler-Röbel (`CR`) against his own FDM methods on the same corpus and reports it losing across the board, with a characteristic non-detection failure that Yang attributes directly to the FM/AM coupling assumption. We already port and beat Yang's FDM, so a CR port would sit two rungs down a chain we have measured both ends of. Porting it would add a row whose ordering is already known from someone else's evaluation, at the cost of reconstructing parameters the authors never released.

**Its core assumption is wrong for our instrument.** The method infers vibrato from frequency modulation inducing amplitude modulation. Attune is violin-first, and bowed-string vibrato is close to pure FM — exactly the case Yang notes the method "cannot be used for". Detecting FM-only vibrato is a requirement of the product, not an edge case, so the one family member that structurally cannot do it is not a competitor we need. Our synthesis makes this worse: the Coco injector produces vibrato by MIDI pitch bend through FluidSynth, which carries almost no AM, so a CR row on Coco would measure our synthesis rather than the method.

The honest treatment is one sentence in the paper naming the family, citing von Coler and Röbel, and stating that it is excluded because it detects vibrato through amplitude coupling that string vibrato does not reliably produce. That is a stronger position than a reconstructed port with guessed thresholds, and it costs nothing. The `Coler2011` audio sitting in `benchmarks/datasets/vibrato/Areas_only/` goes unused as a result; see the note at the end of Part 0.

## Part 0: shared harness changes (do first, once) — complete

The ports need inputs the harness does not currently carry, and three of the four are detection-first methods whose published numbers are F-measures. These changes are prerequisites for every part below.

**0.1 Widen `VibratoExample` — complete.** Add one typed optional field to `benchmarks/modules/vibrato/VibratoBenchmarkTypes.py`: `audio_path: str | None = None`, needed by Driedger. `metadata["audio_path"]` is already populated in both loaders ([CocoVibratoInjector.py:612](../modules/vibrato/CocoVibratoInjector.py#L612), [YangVibratoDataset.py:322](../modules/vibrato/YangVibratoDataset.py#L322)) and just needs promoting to a first-class field. The previously planned `volume` field is no longer needed: von Coler was its only consumer and the family is declined, so per-frame amplitude stays discarded at [CocoVibratoInjector.py:360](../modules/vibrato/CocoVibratoInjector.py#L360). Do not add it speculatively.

**0.2 Add a capability declaration and a real skip path — complete.** Give the `VibratoEstimator` protocol one class-level set, `requires` (`{"pitch"}` or `{"audio"}`). `VibratoBenchmarker.run` should check it against what the example actually carries and emit a row marked skipped rather than invoking the estimator. Today a method that raises lands in `_empty_error_row` and is silently dropped from the summary by the `good = group[group["error"] == ""]` filter at [VibratoBenchmarker.py:512](../modules/vibrato/VibratoBenchmarker.py#L512), which conflates "not applicable to this corpus" with "crashed". The summary needs a `skipped` count beside `errors`. The previously planned `reports` set and its N/A column handling are no longer needed: every remaining method emits detection, rate, and extent, so no cell in the table is inapplicable by construction. That machinery existed only for von Coler, which produced a correlation curve and no parameters.

**0.3 Surface precision and F-measure — complete.** `frame_precision` and `frame_f1` are already computed at [VibratoBenchmarker.py:295](../modules/vibrato/VibratoBenchmarker.py#L295) and [:573](../modules/vibrato/VibratoBenchmarker.py#L573) but dropped from `display_summary`. Add both. The published numbers are mixed and both columns are needed to meet them: Yang reports frame-level F-measure (Eq. 18) *and* rate/extent accuracy (Eq. 19), Driedger reports F-measure (Eq. 7), and Ventura-Sousa-Ferreira reports relative parameter error only. Without precision and F1 surfaced, the detection-side gates cannot be checked at all. Precision is also the column that makes Part 1's regularity gate legible: false alarms alone understate what the gate fixes. Note that this keeps the binary decision entirely frame-wise: every tier already carries a per-frame `is_vibrato` mask, so detection is scored on the same grid as rate and extent, on corpora that also carry parameter truth. That is strictly more informative than an areas-only corpus, because it lets a method be shown to detect well and parameterize badly — which is precisely the Herrera-Bonada and Ventura-Sousa-Ferreira story — and it preserves fair-comparison rule 5's ordering instead of promoting the secondary metric to a table of its own.

**0.4 Decide the timing boundary for audio-domain methods — complete.** The README currently excludes shared audio-to-pitch preparation from `Audio(s)/Compute(s)`. For Driedger the spectrogram *is* the method and must be timed; only file decode should be excluded. Decode once per analysis group, cache the samples on the group, and start the timer after. Record the convention in the README's Metrics section.

**Not doing: an areas-only detection tier.** `benchmarks/datasets/vibrato/Areas_only/` (Coler2011 and CMMSD) carries vibrato *areas* only, with no rate or extent truth, so it can score the binary diagnostic and nothing else. It is deliberately left unused. The claim this harness exists to support is frame-wise accuracy of vibrato parameters that vary *within* a note, and a corpus that can only answer yes/no inverts fair-comparison rule 5 by giving the secondary metric a table of its own. Binary detection is not lost by skipping it — it is already scored frame-wise on every tier (Part 0.3), on corpora that also carry parameter truth. Nor do the ports lose their acceptance gates: Yang's published rate accuracies for both STFT methods are on The Moon Reflected on the Second Spring, which is our existing `Areas_and_parameters` tier, and Driedger gates against his own released dataset. The real cost is coverage — Coler2011 is the only real audio we hold for brass, woodwind, and voice, so dropping it means every real-audio number is erhu, violin, or synthesized. That is an acceptable cost for a violin-first product, but it bounds what the paper may claim across instrument families and should be stated rather than assumed.

## Part 1: Prame (1994, 1997) with Rossignol et al. (1999) decision rules, `prame_rossignol` — complete

New module `benchmarks/modules/vibrato/PrameRossignolExtrema.py`, replacing `ExtremaEstimator` in [VibratoBaselines.py](../modules/vibrato/VibratoBaselines.py#L48). Do this one first: it corrects a row that is already in the results table and being read as a fact about the extrema family, and it builds the interpolated-extrema code that Part 3 reuses. The full audit behind this part is in [EXTREMA_BASELINE_REVIEW.md](EXTREMA_BASELINE_REVIEW.md); only the port specification is repeated here.

**Why the current estimator has to go.** `extrema` is the only method in the harness with no source. Its parameters (35 ms smoothing, 5-cent prominence, 2-15 Hz band) match no published method, and it declares vibrato from a single isolated half-cycle with no regularity or duration requirement, where every other method in the harness has one. Measured on synthetic mixed material, that costs a 0.466 false-alarm rate; adding Rossignol's own decision rule to the same extrema drops it to 0.038 at unchanged recall. Under fair-comparison rule 6 it is currently an unlabelled reproduction of nothing, so a weak extrema row cannot be attributed to the family rather than to us. It is also a fairness problem for the paper, since Attune's own step-3 rate seed uses an amplitude-relative hysteresis threshold and a six-extrema support rule that the baseline lacks.

**Sources.** Prame, "Measurements of the vibrato rate of ten singers", JASA 96(4), 1994, 1979-1984; Prame, "Vibrato extent and intonation in professional Western lyric singing", JASA 102(1), 1997, 616-621; Rossignol, Depalle, Soumagne, Rodet, and Collette, "Vibrato: detection, estimation, extraction, modification", DAFx-99, section 2.5. No released implementation exists for either, so this is an independent reproduction and must be labeled as one under fair-comparison rule 6.

**The parameter convention, from Prame.** Rate is the inverse of the interval between successive extrema; extent is the peak-to-trough difference in cents; intonation is the extrema midline. Our current code already computes rate as `0.5 / half_period` and extent as the peak-to-trough difference, which is this convention, so the arithmetic carries over unchanged. Worth stating explicitly in the paper: this is also the convention Yang's manual annotation uses ("assuming the interval between one peak and one trough is the duration of a half cycle, and the vibrato rate is the inverse of the cycle length"), so on the `Areas_and_parameters` tier the baseline and the ground truth compute the same quantity by construction, which makes this row that tier's natural floor.

**The decision rule, from Rossignol section 2.5.** Local maxima of the f0 trajectory are detected and **precisely pinpointed by interpolation**. All temporal distances between successive local maxima are computed, then their **variance** and the **count** falling in 0.15-0.25 s (4-6.7 Hz). The same is done independently for the local minima. Vibrato is declared when both variances are low **and** both counts are high — a regularity test across many extrema, not a per-cycle test. Significance is confirmed by a large `Mfreq = mean((max_interp - min_interp) / ((max_interp + min_interp) / 2))`. The midline `(max_interp + min_interp) / 2` is the by-product estimate of the vibrato-free contour and is what the method reports as pitch center. f0-trajectory methods in that paper use 300 ms windows.

**What changes against the current code, in order of impact.** Add the regularity gate, which does not exist today. Add parabolic interpolation of both extremum time and value; the current code reads integer-grid times, which puts up to 3% error on every rate at our frame geometry before noise. Replace the AVA MIDI-0 unvoiced sentinel with `_filled()`, which already exists at [VibratoBaselines.py:34](../modules/vibrato/VibratoBaselines.py#L34) and is dead code, and report `filled_unvoiced_frames` in metadata as the McLeod port does at [McLeodTartiniProny.py:182](../modules/vibrato/McLeodTartiniProny.py#L182) — two estimators in one harness using opposite unvoiced policies are not comparable. Take width from the interpolated extremum values rather than from the smoothed contour. Take center from the extrema midline rather than a 0.45 s Savitzky-Golay. Replace the invented `qualities` expression with `Mfreq` and the inverse interval variance.

**The threshold decision, which is the interesting part.** Rossignol defers his variance and count cutoffs to the companion journal article (Rossignol, Rodet, Soumagne, Collette, and Depalle, JNMR 1999), so a port must choose them. Try that article first; if it is not obtainable, choose cutoffs and label them adapter behavior, the same way Part 4 handles Driedger's unspecified parameters. The cutoffs used in the review measurement were at least four cycles per run, coefficient of variation of half-cycle intervals at most 0.25, and a majority of half-cycles in band. Rate band defaults to Rossignol's published 4-6.7 Hz, with a `band_mode="native" | "harness"` switch exposing the wider band, mirroring the `window_mode` decision in Part 2. Report both if they differ materially.

**Validation.** There is no published accuracy number for an extrema detector to gate against — Yang's comparison contains no extrema method, and Prame reported population statistics rather than estimator error. So validation is synthetic recovery of a 5.5 Hz / 80-cent contour to within 0.1 Hz and 5%, following the existing tests in `test_vibrato_benchmark.py`, then the frame-wise numbers on the Yang `Areas_and_parameters` tier, whose half-cycle annotation is this method's own convention. [probe_extrema_gates.py](probe_extrema_gates.py) is the existing mixed-material probe and should become a regression test.

**Naming.** Ship as `prame_rossignol`; keep `extrema` as a deprecated alias so existing notebook cells resolve, and remove it after the next full run.

## Part 2: Herrera and Bonada (1998), `herrera_bonada_stft` — complete

New module `benchmarks/modules/vibrato/HerreraBonadaSTFT.py`, following the `YangAVAFDM.py` house pattern: a source-faithful numerical core, a provenance docstring, and clearly separated adapter behavior.

**Source.** [dafx98-perfe.pdf](http://www.mtg.upf.edu/files/publications/dafx98-perfe.pdf), Section 2. There is no released implementation, so this is an independent reproduction and must be labeled as one under fair-comparison rule 6.

**The algorithm, as specified by the authors.** The f0 track arrives at 345 frames per second — which is within 0.15% of Attune's own 344.53 Hz grid (44100/128), so no resampling adapter is needed, unlike the McLeod port. Subtract the track's global mean to get a 0-centered series. Window at **128 points (0.371 s) with 50% overlap**; the authors tested Blackman-Harris, Kaiser, and Hamming and report no significant difference, so pick one and record it. FFT each window, locate spectral peaks by **parabolic interpolation**, and take the most prominent peak; a region has vibrato when that peak sits around 5-6 Hz. Rate and depth are stored per window and pooled over a region.

**The window-geometry decision, which is the interesting part.** Yang re-implemented HB at *his* comparison geometry (0.125 s, hop w/4) rather than HB's own 0.371 s, and then reported that HB labels almost every frame as vibrato "likely due to the low frequency resolution of the FFT at the short window size". At 0.125 s an FFT resolves 8 Hz; at HB's native 0.371 s it resolves 2.7 Hz. Implement **both** and default to native: `window_mode="native"` (0.371 s / 50%) as the authors published it, and `window_mode="yang_comparison"` (0.125 s / 25% hop) to reproduce Yang Figure 5(a). Expect these to give materially different precision, and report both. If native HB is not the straw man its reputation suggests, that is a finding and it belongs in the paper.

**Adapter behavior to document explicitly.** Extent scaling from FFT magnitude to cents (`2|X[k]|/Σw`, then semitones to cents). Mapping sparse window centers onto the shared frame grid — reuse the existing AVA adapter mapper rather than writing a second one. Unvoiced-frame policy: HB assumes a delimited steady-state region and says nothing about gaps, so use the interpolate-then-flag policy shared with McLeod and Part 1. Decision band: use the same `Fthd` the FDM decision tree uses, exactly as Yang did when he made thresholds uniform across methods.

**Note the framing caveat.** HB explicitly presumes "a steady state has been correctly delimitated" in a prior analysis stage. Run it on the full contour like every other method (harness rule 2), but say in the paper that this is more adversarial than its original setting.

**Validation.** Unit test: recover a synthetic 5.5 Hz / 80-cent sinusoid to within 0.1 Hz and 5%, following the existing synthetic-recovery tests in `test_vibrato_benchmark.py`. Corpus test: Yang's rate accuracies for HB on The Moon Reflected on the Second Spring are 92.68% and 87.99% for the two erhu performers, and that recording is our existing `Areas_and_parameters` tier, so the `yang_comparison` mode should land near those numbers on a tier we already have. With Part 0.3's precision column in place, the same run should also reproduce HB's published signature of high recall with low precision, frame-wise.

## Part 3: Ventura, Sousa, and Ferreira (2012), `ventura_sousa_ferreira` — complete

Same module family as Part 2 — put it in `VenturaSousaFerreiraSTFT.py` rather than inside `HerreraBonadaSTFT.py`, since the house rule is one file per class and the two differ in more than a peak estimator once their window geometries are honoured. Do it after HB so the shared STFT-of-f0 scaffolding and the sparse-to-dense grid mapper already exist.

**Why both STFT members and not just HB.** Yang evaluates both and finds them close in aggregate F-measure but opposite in error structure: HB tends to split vibratos and miss some, VSF sits "at the opposite end of the spectrum", merging and over-detecting, which Yang attributes to the different peak-picking mechanisms. Reporting only HB would represent the family by its conservative end and make the family look uniformly split-prone. Their geometries also differ by roughly a factor of four in window length, which is the tradeoff the family is actually parameterized by.

**Source.** [Ventura, Sousa, and Ferreira, "Accurate analysis and visual feedback of vibrato in singing", ISCCSP 2012](https://fe.up.pt/voicestudies/artts/doc/publications/086%20-%20ACCURATE%20ANALYSIS%20AND%20VISUAL%20FEEDBACK%20OF%20VIBRATO%20IN%20SINGING.pdf), Section 3. Freely available. No released implementation; independent reproduction, fair-comparison rule 6.

**The algorithm, as specified by the authors.** Convert the f0 contour to the log/MIDI scale first — they argue vibrato is more faithfully sinusoidal there than in linear frequency, which is the same reason Attune works in semitones. Then, per analysis step: take a segment of **64 samples** from the f0 contour; remove the DC component to minimize leakage; compute the magnitude spectrum; obtain a spectral envelope model by short-pass liftering the real cepstrum, preserving the first four cepstrum bins and their negative-axis replicas; detect the largest peak **within bins 6 to 12**; evaluate the dB difference between that peak and both the noise floor and the spectral-envelope floor; declare vibrato when that difference exceeds **3.8 dB**; compute the rate by the frequency interpolation method of Sousa and Ferreira (2010); and compute the extent in semitones as **the average difference between f0 maxima and minima within the segment**.

**The geometry, which must be resampled and is the mirror image of HB's.** Their f0 contour runs at 43 Hz (1024-point FFT, 50% overlap, 22050 Hz), not our 344.53 Hz. Resample to their native 43 Hz grid and map back, exactly as the McLeod port resamples to Tartini's grid; do not run a 64-point FFT on our grid. The arithmetic confirms the reading: 64 points at 43 Hz gives a 0.672 Hz bin, so bins 6-12 are 4.03-8.06 Hz, which is precisely the "4 Hz till 8 Hz" range the authors state. Note what this implies — a 64-sample window at 43 Hz is a **1.49 s** analysis window, four times HB's 0.371 s and twelve times AVA's 0.125 s. VSF buys its accuracy with time resolution, and their own hop of 6 samples (0.14 s) does not recover it. That is the finding this port exists to produce, and it is exactly the weakness Attune's whole-note time-varying model is arguing against, so state it plainly rather than burying it in a table.

**Note that the extent is an extrema measurement.** "The average difference between f0 maxima and minima within the current segment" is Prame's convention applied inside an FFT-detected segment. Reuse Part 1's interpolated-extrema helper rather than writing a second one; this is the concrete reason to sequence Part 1 before Part 3. It also means VSF's extent column and the `prame_rossignol` extent column share machinery and should agree closely where both detect, which is a useful internal consistency check.

**The one unobtainable piece, and why it is low risk.** The peak estimator is Sousa and Ferreira, "Non-iterative frequency estimation in the DFT magnitude domain", ISCCSP 2010, which is IEEE-paywalled; the follow-on "DFT-based frequency estimation under harmonic interference" may restate it and is worth trying first. If neither is obtainable, substitute any accurate interpolator (parabolic on log-magnitude, or Quinn/Jacobsen) and label it adapter behavior. Unlike the von Coler situation, this substitution cannot plausibly change a scored result: the authors' own claim is a maximum error of about 0.1% of the bin width, which at this geometry is roughly 7e-4 Hz against a 0.5 Hz rate tolerance. The parameters that actually determine VSF's behaviour — the 64-point window, bins 6-12, the four-bin lifter, and the 3.8 dB threshold — are all published. Record the substitution in the docstring and move on; do not treat it as a blocker.

**Validation.** Their synthetic tests are reproducible directly: vibrato rate swept 4-8 Hz in 0.5 Hz steps at F0 = 440 Hz with extents 0.5 and 1.0 ST, and extent swept 0.2-1.2 ST at F0 = 261.6 Hz with rate 5 Hz. Published relative errors are under 0.1% for rate (means 0.057% and 0.082%). Reproducing that on synthetic contours gates the module. Corpus check: Yang's VSF rate accuracies on the erhu performers are 86.08% and 76.86%, below HB's — so if our VSF outscores our HB on a comparable tier, something is wrong with one of the two ports.

## Part 4: Driedger, Balke, Ewert, and Müller (2016), `driedger_template` — implementation complete; acceptance incomplete

New module `benchmarks/modules/vibrato/DriedgerTemplateVibrato.py`. Highest value of the ports: it is the family that most directly challenges our framing, and it is the only one with published per-item numbers we can hit.

**Source.** [ISMIR 2016 paper 000005](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2016/paper/000005.pdf), Section 2. The [AudioLabs resource page](https://www.audiolabs-erlangen.de/resources/MIR/2016-ISMIR-Vibrato) releases the audio and annotations as a zip but **no code**, so this is an independent reproduction from the paper's equations.

**The algorithm.** STFT of the signal, then a phase-vocoder-reassigned log-frequency spectrogram (their reference is Müller FMP Chapter 8) at roughly **150 frames/s and 10 bands per semitone**, so that a vibrato pattern's amplitude directly reflects its extent regardless of which overtone it sits on. Binarize by setting the **top ten percent of each frame** to one and everything else to zero, giving `Y`. Build templates from `s(t) = e·sin(2πft)` over `t ∈ [0, ℓ]`, discretized onto Y's grid: bins close to `s` get positive values, bins at a certain distance get negative values, the rest zero, with positive entries normalized to sum to +1 and negative entries to −1. Vibrato salience is `S_T(m,k) = max over shifts (μ,κ) that map (m,k) onto a positive template entry, of c(T, Y^{(μ,κ)})`, then `S(m,k) = max over T of S_T(m,k)`. A frame is vibrato when its maximum salience exceeds `τ = 0.55`. Per-frame rate and extent are the parameters of the salience-maximizing template.

**The efficiency trick, without which this is unusable.** The paper describes a quadruply nested loop and concedes it is expensive. `c(T, Y^{(μ,κ)})` evaluated over all shifts is exactly the 2-D cross-correlation of `Y` with `T`, computable in one `scipy.signal.fftconvolve(Y, T[::-1, ::-1])`. A literal implementation can then obtain Equation 5 by grayscale dilation. The benchmark only consumes `max_m S_T(m,k)`, however, so the finite maxima can be reordered: compute edge-aware frequency-range maxima of the correlation for each positive support offset, shift those one-dimensional traces in time, and maximize them. Regression tests compare this reduction with the literal dilation at frequency boundaries. This preserves every template and shift while avoiding the full salience image that made `grey_dilation` dominate runtime. The paper also suggests a limited frequency range and threshold heuristics, but does not publish code or say which bank produced its 60 s audio / 40 s MATLAB timing.

**Parameters the paper does not specify, which we must choose and record as adapter behavior.** The STFT frame length and hop; the log-frequency range and total band count; the two distance thresholds that define the positive and negative bands in template construction; and whether the per-frame salience maximum spans the whole frequency axis or a restricted range. Fix these by matching the stated resolutions (150 fps, 10 bands/semitone) and by tuning **only** against their released dataset until the port reproduces their published per-item F-measures, then freeze before touching any of our corpora. That ordering is fair-comparison rule 1 and matters more here than for any other port, because these are our choices standing in for theirs.

**Template sets.** The paper uses 30 templates for its quantitative detection benchmark (5-7 Hz in 0.5 Hz steps, 50-100 cents in 10 cent steps, ℓ = 0.4 s) and a larger 285-template set only for its short parameter-analysis demonstration (4-11 Hz in 0.5 Hz steps, 30-210 cents in 10 cent steps). `driedger_template` defaults to the published 30-template bank for every benchmark suite; the analysis bank remains opt-in for diagnostics. This intentionally limits reported rate/extent support to the source benchmark's grid rather than silently turning the demonstration bank into the detector. The resulting quantization and unsupported Attune edge profiles are findings, not defects to interpolate away. `driedger_template_benchmark_range` is now an explicitly labeled companion ablation: its 165-template strict-superset bank spans Attune's 3-10 Hz and 10-200 cent full-width benchmark support while retaining the same threshold and adapter geometry. Keeping this as a second row measures the coverage/multiple-comparisons tradeoff without misattributing the expanded bank to Driedger et al.

**Implemented unit adapter.** Equation 2 and Figure 3 show that Driedger's `e` is the one-sided deviation (`e=50` spans -50 to +50 cents). The estimator therefore draws the template at +/-`e` and reports `2e` into `VibratoEstimate.width_cents`, whose contract is full peak-to-peak width.

**Harness fit.** Declares `requires={"audio"}`. Runs on Coco and Yang; skipped on the synthetic contour tier, which has no audio at all. Its acceptance gate is its authors' own released dataset, which is independent of our tiers either way.

**Validation.** Download their zip to `benchmarks/datasets/vibrato_driedger/` and reproduce Table 1: per-item F-measures with means of **0.80 (−0 dB), 0.77 (−5 dB), 0.76 (−10 dB)**. Treat within roughly 0.05 of the published means as acceptance. This is the strongest validation available for any port in this plan and should gate the module before it is added to `default_estimators()`.

**Expect it to lose on our corpora, and frame that honestly.** Our audio is clean, solo, and monophonic — the condition in which their own Table 1 shows the F0-based upper bound beating them (0.87 vs 0.80). Their advantage appears at −10 dB, where F0-based detection collapses to 0.25 and they hold 0.76. We do not currently test any polyphonic condition, so the honest statement is that the method's robustness is real and our corpus does not exercise it.

## Part 5: notebook integration — complete

Minimal. `benchmarks/vibrato/vibrato.ipynb` cell 2 defines a single `BENCHMARK_METHODS` tuple that feeds `--methods` for every suite, so adding the new names there is the whole change and every existing cell picks them up on rerun. Every suite in the notebook is audio-backed (Coco and Yang both carry `audio_path`), so no suite needs a different method list, and no new suite cell is needed. When `extrema` is renamed in Part 1, update the tuple and leave the alias in place for one run so old cached outputs stay readable.

Adding a method to `available_estimators()` in `VibratoBaselines.py` is sufficient for `--methods name` to resolve; no CLI changes are needed. Whether each new method joins `default_estimators()` is a separate call — recommend keeping each out of the default set until its validation gate passes. `prame_rossignol` is the exception: it replaces a method already in the default set, so it should enter the default set as soon as its synthetic-recovery test passes, and `extrema` should leave at the same moment rather than both appearing in one table.

## Part 6: sequencing and risk

Recommended order:

1. **Part 0** in full. Nothing else can be checked in cleanly without the capability/skip machinery and the F-measure columns.
2. **Prame and Rossignol.** First among the ports: it is the cheapest, it corrects a number that is already being read as a result, and it builds the interpolated-extrema helper that Part 3 depends on.
3. **Herrera and Bonada.** Fully specified, no external blockers, and it establishes the STFT-of-f0 module and adapter pattern Part 3 reuses. The native-vs-Yang window comparison is a paper result on its own.
4. **Ventura, Sousa, and Ferreira.** Directly after HB while the scaffolding is fresh. Cheap given Parts 1 and 2, and it completes the STFT family so the split/merge contrast is reportable.
5. **Driedger.** Last, because it is the largest implementation and the only one with a hard external acceptance gate. Do it while there is time to hit that gate rather than rushing it.

Risks worth tracking. Rossignol's variance and count cutoffs may stay unobtainable, in which case they ship as documented adapter choices and must be frozen before any scored run — the gate is the single largest determinant of that row, so a cutoff tuned after seeing our results would be the worst kind of unfairness. Sousa and Ferreira's peak estimator is paywalled, but the substitution is provably immaterial at our tolerance and should not stall Part 3. Driedger's unspecified parameters may not reach the published F-measures, in which case report the gap rather than tuning on our own corpora to close it. And three of the four are detection-first methods being scored by a curve-first harness, so Part 0.3's precision and F-measure columns need to land before any of the numbers are quotable.
